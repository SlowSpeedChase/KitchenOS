# Completed: plates

**Completed:** 2026-08-02
**Branch:** `plates` (merged `ec0ede0`)
**Duration:** same day

## Summary

Two things: the first 16 curated **plates** — main-plus-accompaniment meal bundles — seeded
into `vault/KitchenOS/Meals/`, and the `_is_pantry` defect that surfaced while measuring
whether the existing plate composer was fit to generate them. It wasn't, which is why the
plates are hand-authored.

## Why the plates are authored, not generated

`scripts/compose_plates.py` pairs a main with accompaniments by ingredient overlap. Measured
against the live library before the fix below, its output was degenerate: **62% of pairings
rested on a single shared ingredient**, 53% on `garlic cloves` alone, and one recipe (*Jammy
Zucchini*) was proposed as the side for **31 of 76 paired mains** — every one of them on
garlic and nothing else. A page built on that would have said "serve zucchini with
everything."

So the plates were paired on cooking judgment — cuisine, what the side does for the main
(contrast, starch, acid), shared oven or pan time — and each note carries a
`## Why this plate` section arguing the pairing, so a plate can be argued with rather than
just deleted.

**They are hand-authored artifacts, in the `My Meal System.md` category, not the
`Inventory.md` category.** No script regenerates them and nothing should overwrite them. The
one-off seed script was deliberately left in the session scratchpad rather than committed, so
that a future "regenerate plates" command cannot exist by accident.

## The defect: `_is_pantry`

`garlic` is a declared staple in `config/pantry_staples.json`, but `_is_pantry` matched a
staple exactly or as a **suffix** — `"kosher salt".endswith(" salt")`. The mirror case was
open: `garlic cloves` is the staple plus a form noun, so it survived the pantry filter and
counted as genuine shared shopping.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Pairings driven by `garlic cloves` | 77 of 144 | **0** |
| Single-ingredient pairings | 89 of 144 (62%) | 49 of 106 (46%) |
| *Jammy Zucchini* proposed as a side | 31 mains | **0** |

The trailing form is restricted to an explicit noun list (`clove`, `head`, `bulb`, `leaf`,
`sprig`, `stalk`) rather than "any one extra word", because `garlic powder` and `garlic
bread` are different foods — the compound-food trap the audit files under Phase 4. Verified
live after the LaunchAgent restart: `garlic cloves` → 0 matches, `garlic powder` → 39,
`chickpeas, cumin` → 46.

The scorer is shared by `use_it_up`, `cook_now` and `POST /api/recipes/by-ingredients`, so
all four were inflated, not just the composer.

3880 tests pass; ruff parity with main (4 = 4, pre-existing).

## Lessons learned

**A verification that returns zero for everything is not a verification.** The first
functional check read `d["recipes"]` where the endpoint returns `matches`, so every query —
including a control that should have matched 46 recipes — came back empty and briefly looked
like a clean pass. The control case is what caught it.

**The composer's weakness was only visible against the real library.** Its unit tests pass;
the pairings are only obviously degenerate when you count how often one side wins.

## Still open

- **A meal card contributes nothing to a board week's day-totals row.** Meals are
  deliberately not serving-ledger citizens (documented in CLAUDE.md), so a plate shows its
  macros on `/api/meals` and the recipe page but not in the planner totals. Phase 5 decision C
  — "keep composite meals, which now means making them ledger-native" — is the work that
  closes it, and these 16 plates are now a concrete reason to do it.
- **`compose_plates.py` is still CLI-only** and still unwired to any surface. With the staple
  fix its output is materially better (single-ingredient pairings 62% → 46%) but it is not yet
  good enough to author plates unsupervised.
- The plates cover 16 of ~70 eligible mains. Widening is cheap now that the pattern exists.
